Placement — vertical Jager heaters

Plan view, back wall at top. A vertical heater reads as a circle here — you're looking down the cylinder. The spray bar discharges forward across the full width; water runs down the front glass, along the bottom, and returns to the intake at back-right. All three options keep every piece of hardware against the back wall. The difference is lateral position, and it decides whether your two probes are measuring the tank or measuring your mounting choices.

Why the probes move inboard rather than outboard

The obvious alternative — push each probe out toward its side wall — fails on the right, because that corner is the intake. A probe there reads water on its way out of the tank, which is the least representative sample available. Inboard keeps both probes in equivalent flow and preserves the mirror.

Vertical costs you almost nothing. Horizontal-and-low would have added convection assist through the water column, but a 528 gph canister through a full-width spray bar is doing far more mixing than natural convection ever would. What actually mattered — inboard of the dead corners, mirrored about centre, clear of the intake — all survives.

Two Jager limits to check against your tank: water must never drop below the minimum mark on the housing, and maximum immersion depth is 0.5 m / 19.7″. On a tank taller than about 20″ that means the heater body hangs in the upper water column rather than reaching the substrate — which suits this layout fine, since it puts the elements right in the spray bar's discharge.
